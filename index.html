01Why AI

The hard part isn't using AI. It's making it dependable when the world is messy.

Two years in banking taught me that a system is only as good as its data. During a six-month merger of two banks, I helped migrate and validate sensitive customer records, where a small error is expensive.

My route started with a Computer Science degree at Southampton, where my First-Class dissertation looked at object recognition under partial occlusion. Since then I've built enterprise systems at AstroBank and Alpha Bank that staff use every day. They combine C# and APIs with low-code platforms like Appian and Power Apps, backed by SQL that handles heavy load and high request volumes.

I started the MSc in AI because I was intrigued by the topic and found that it matched my interests and curiosities closely. The field moves fast, and I don't want the wave to go on without me. I want to build and test things myself rather than watch from the sidelines.

“Most vision systems are evaluated on clean data. I'm more interested in what happens when the object is half-hidden, the scene is noisy, or the process has to run inside a bank.” Working principle
01

Test where it breaks

Clean benchmarks show what a model can do, not whether you can rely on it.

02

Check the data first

A model fed bad records fails quietly, and in banking quiet failures are the expensive ones.

03

Say what it can't do

A clear limit is more useful than a confident number.
