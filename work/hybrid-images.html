Computer Vision · 2023

An image is more than one frequency.

A computer-vision study of convolution, spatial filtering, and hybrid images, and a reminder that perception depends on distance.

Year
2023
Context
Computer Vision coursework
Role
Individual
Stack
Python · NumPy · OpenCV

Overview

Hybrid images combine a low-frequency image with the high-frequency detail of another. Up close you see one thing; from across the room, another. The project was a practical way to understand convolution and the information different spatial frequencies carry.

It sits at the foundation of computer vision. Before you can reason about a CNN, it helps to know what a filter actually does to an image.

Approach

01 / Low-pass

Apply a Gaussian blur to keep the broad structure and remove fine detail. What remains is the low-frequency signal: shape, colour, and large-scale contrast.

02 / High-pass

Subtract the low-pass result from the original to isolate edges and texture. What remains is the part of the image that changes quickly across space.

03 / Combine and inspect

Add the two signals together and view the result at different scales. The interesting part is not just the output image; it is how your perception of it changes as the image gets smaller.

What I built

  • A filtering pipeline for Gaussian blur and high-pass extraction.
  • A hybrid-image generator with adjustable frequency cutoffs.
  • Visual comparisons showing the same image at multiple scales.
  • Notes on how filter size and cutoff affect the final perception.

Outcome

The project made convolution tactile rather than abstract. It built intuition for why edge and detail information and global structure are processed differently, useful when reasoning about CNNs, detection, and why a model can recognise an object at one resolution but not another.
