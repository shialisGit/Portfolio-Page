Computer Vision · 2024

Teaching a detector to see what's partially there.

My First-Class dissertation asked a narrow question with an awkward answer: how do you build and evaluate an object-recognition system when occlusion is the default, not the exception?

Year
2024
Context
BSc dissertation · First-Class
Role
Individual research
Stack

Overview

Most computer-vision benchmarks are built around clear views. A pedestrian is fully visible; a car is not parked behind a van. Real scenes are less cooperative. Occlusion is one of the main reasons a model that looks strong in a notebook can fail in a deployment.

Rather than treating occlusion as noise, I treated it as the design constraint. The dissertation combined three pieces: a generator for synthetic occluded scenes, an interactive application for running and inspecting YOLOv8, and an evaluation of how detection and segmentation behave as objects become progressively hidden.

Approach

01 / Build the data first

Public occlusion datasets are limited. I built a pipeline that extracts objects, removes their backgrounds, and composites them into new scenes with controlled overlap, scale, and position. That made occlusion a variable I could tune rather than an accident I had to find.

02 / Test the detector, not just the demo

I wrapped YOLOv8 in a Streamlit app so I could run detection and segmentation on images and video and inspect the failure cases directly, not just read aggregate scores. Moving from a checkpoint to a visual answer needed to be fast.

03 / Treat evaluation as part of the system

I compared clean and occluded conditions and documented where confidence, boundaries, and masks degraded. The useful output wasn't a single number; it was a map of failure modes.

The most important lesson was that data design is model design. Changing what the model sees during training changed what it could learn far more than tweaking a demo.

What I built

  • A synthetic dataset pipeline for object extraction, background removal, and scene compositing.
  • A Streamlit interface for image and video inference with detection boxes and segmentation masks.
  • Evaluation notes comparing clean and occluded scenes, with a focus on where the model broke down.
Dataset generator ↗ YOLOv8 app ↗

Outcome

The dissertation was awarded a First-Class grade. More importantly, it gave me a working understanding of the gap between benchmark performance and deployment behaviour, and a habit of asking what the training data never showed the model.

The dataset generator and the YOLO testing app became the two standalone projects that follow this case study.
